2 WHILE YOU WATCH

Watch the documentary video (or listen to the audio version below).

Full video: Empower Advanced Documentary Video Unit 01 (hieroglyphs & emoji exhibition).

a Watch the video. Answer the questions.

1 How are hieroglyphs similar to emojis?
2 What is the main difference between them?
3 In what way are emojis similar to a pidgin language?

Possible: 1 picture writing / express meaning through pictures. 2 Hieroglyphs = complete writing system; emoji = shorthand for simple concepts. 3 simple strategies, understood across communities with no common language.

b Watch again. Make notes about the topics below.

1 The curator’s aim
2 Becoming a scribe in ancient Egypt
3 The invention of emojis
4 Communicating complex ideas in emojis
5 How to say ‘very’ in a pidgin language
6 Emoji translators
7 Emojis in Europe, the USA and other countries
8 The future of emojis

c Complete the sentences with the word that matches the meaning in italics. Watch again and check your answers.

1 Scribes were the only people in ancient Egypt who were (given official permission) to use hieroglyphics.
2 The exhibition shows the (surprising and easily noticed) similarities between hieroglyphs and emojis.
3 There are also (important and great) differences between the two systems.
4 A smiley face emoji can signify (not trusting someone) or (not believing someone) in China.
5 How our use of emojis will (gradually change) in the years ahead is unknown.

d Work in pairs. The curator’s aim in showing hieroglyphs and emojis together in the museum exhibition was to show how ancient Egyptian culture is still relevant today. Answer the questions. Give reasons for your answers.

1 Do you think the approach was a good idea?
2 Think of arguments … for the approach · against it
3 Which argument do you think is the most convincing?

3 AFTER YOU WATCH

a Think about a museum or an exhibition you have visited. Make notes.

1 Where and when was it? What did it show?
2 Did you enjoy it? What rating would you give it from 1 (the least) to 5 (the most)? Why?
3 What do you remember about it?
4 What, if anything, do you think was done to make it … more interesting or attractive to look at · relevant to your life

b Work in pairs. Tell your partner about the museum or exhibition you visited. Ask your partner questions about their experience.

Speaking task – use your notes from 3a. Optional: record yourself with the device microphone if desired.
